Reactor Core Explorer

A toy nuclear reactor core. Slide the control rods and the moderator to push the chain reaction above or below critical (k = 1) and watch the neutron population respond. Fire more neutrons with the mouse.

A model in arbitrary units. It is not a real reactor.

This page shows a 2D reactor core. Yellow rings are fuel, blue dots are moderator atoms, gray squares are control rods, and small colored dots are neutrons, blue when slow and red when fast. A slow neutron that hits fuel usually causes a fission, which releases two or three fast neutrons. Moderator atoms slow neutrons down, and control rods absorb them. The control panel and the diagnostics panel give every number as text, and every mouse action also has a keyboard route.

Live diagnostics

How to Read the Diagnostics: keff is neutrons made by fission divided by neutrons lost (to fission, rods, leaks) over the last 4 seconds. Above 1, the population grows, and below 1 it shrinks. The neutron balance is exac, so neutrons in minus neutrons out must equal the count. The energy ledger books every flow the engine cannot see (absorption, fission, the heat bath, the source, your mouse), so the ledger error measures the solver and not the reactions. It turns red past 1 %.

Limits: 2D only, circles only, arbitrary units, no heat feedback and no delayed neutrons. Neutrons never collide with each other. A speed limit of 20 m/s stops tunneling, and a cap of 400 neutrons protects the frame rate, so a runaway chain stops growing at the cap.
