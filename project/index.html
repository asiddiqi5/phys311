This page shows a 2D reactor core. Yellow rings are fuel, blue dots are moderator atoms, gray squares are control rods, and small colored dots are neutrons, blue when slow and red when fast. A slow neutron that hits fuel usually causes a fission, which releases two or three fast neutrons. Moderator atoms slow neutrons down, and control rods absorb them. What am I looking at?

A reactor makes heat by splitting atoms. Four things are on the canvas:

Neutrons (small dots) split. Their colour is their speed: red is fast, blue is slow.
Fuel (yellow rings) is the uranium. A neutron splitting a fuel atom is a fission, meaning it releases heat and throws out two or three new neutrons, which split more atoms. That is a chain reaction.
Moderator (blue dots) is the water. Fast neutrons are bad at causing fission and slow ones are good at it, so its job is to slow neutrons down. Turn it off and the chain reaction dies, even with plenty of fuel.
Control rods (grey squares) swallow any neutron that touches them, which is how a reactor is turned up and down.

The Most Important Variable is K

For every neutron that disappears, how many new ones are made? That ratio is k. Below 1 the reaction ceases to produce neutrons. At 1, the power is steady, and is "critical" meaning the normal running state of every power reactor. Above 1, the population grows. The chart uses a log scale, so steady growth or decay is a straight line.

Why Temperature Matters

Hot fuel fissions slightly less readily than cold fuel. That is why reactors are controllable; as the power climbs, the fuel heats, and the heat pushes k back down on its own, so the reactor settles by itself.

Try These

  • Pull the rods out and watch the population climb, then settle as the fuel heats.
  • Drag the moderator to zero. The chain reaction dies even though the fuel is untouched.
  • Press S for SCRAM, the emergency shutdown that drops every rod at once.
  • Turn the temperature feedback off and pull the rods out.
  • Drag on empty space to launch your own neutron, and aim it at a fuel ring.

This is a toy, which is made up of fake units, two dimensions, and far less going on than a real reactor.

How to Read the Diagnostics: keff is neutrons made by fission divided by neutrons lost (to fission, rods, leaks) over the last 4 seconds. Above 1, the population grows, and below 1 it shrinks. The neutron balance is exac, so neutrons in minus neutrons out must equal the count. The energy ledger books every flow the engine cannot see (absorption, fission, the heat bath, the source, your mouse), so the ledger error measures the solver and not the reactions. It turns red past 1 %.

Fuel Temperature. Each fission heats the fuel, and the coolant carries heat away. Hot fuel fissions less readily, so k falls as the core heats and the reactor settles at a steady power on its own. Turn the feedback off and nothing but the neutron cap stops a runaway.

Limits: 2D only, circles only, arbitrary units, and no delayed neutrons. Neutrons never collide with each other. A speed limit of 20 m/s stops tunneling, and a cap of 400 neutrons protects the frame rate, so a runaway chain stops growing at the cap.
